Literature review · design input for quorum

Axes of Agentic Engineering

Thirty projects and fifteen papers across four families, placed on eleven axes of orchestration design — and where quorum sits on each.

Method — four parallel research agents, primary sources fetched 2026-08-29 (official docs, repos, changelogs, arXiv). Families: general-purpose agent frameworks · coding-agent orchestrators · harness-native orchestration · research literature. Quorum's own placements are checked against docs/architecture.md, not paraphrased from memory.

Headline findings

F1

All three of quorum's invariants survive contact with the landscape — strengthened, not weakened. No recorded stance needs revision. The roadmap this survey produces is additive: enrichments within the current design, not reversals of it.

F2

Orchestration is moving into the harnesses, and that helps rather than hurts. Every major harness shipped native subagents in a twelve-month window, and five of six ship vendor-cloud detached execution. But the same wave made every session an addressable, steerable, forkable object (hooks, stream protocols, control-plane APIs) — exactly the interface an external supervisor needs. The external layer's defensible value is now precisely quorum's positioning: cross-harness, local-first, policy-owned.

F3

On supervision, quorum sits with the frontier commercial products, not its OSS peers. Open-source orchestrators "supervise" with keystroke automation (Claude Squad's TapEnter daemon, uzi's blind auto-confirm). Only Factory's Mission Control and Devin's coordinator (spawns, messages, budget-caps, and kills child Devins) crossed into LLM supervision — the pattern quorum's manager already implements locally.

F4

Worktrees won the local isolation war; kernel sandboxes went mainstream. Every surviving local orchestrator isolates with a worktree per task; Sculptor conspicuously reverted from container-per-agent to worktrees in 2026. Seatbelt/bubblewrap+seccomp sandboxing now ships inside Codex, Claude Code, and Factory. Quorum's worktrees + fail-closed nono are squarely on trend.

F5

The literature converged: coding is the canonical bad case for fine-grained multi-agent decomposition. Anthropic and Cognition — publishing opposite manifestos in the same week of June 2025 — agree on this. Coarse-grained parallelism (independent tasks in isolated worktrees) sidesteps the objection entirely, which is the parallelism quorum sells.

F6

Agents do not self-detect failure, so verification must be external and executable. A 20,574-session field study (2026): 91.5% of agent–user misalignments needed explicit human correction, and self-reporting accuracy is worsening. Quorum's stranded-work probe (git state over reported status) is the right instinct; the survey says extend it, especially toward CI.

AXIS 01

Agent-loop ownership

HOLD

Who owns the think–act loop. Every general-purpose framework surveyed (LangGraph, Microsoft Agent Framework, CrewAI, OpenAI Agents SDK, smolagents, Mastra, Pydantic AI) builds and ships its own loop; none wraps a third-party harness CLI. The vocabulary shifted in quorum's direction, though: OpenAI, Microsoft, and Pydantic all independently adopted the word "harness" as a named architectural layer in 2026, and the Claude Agent SDK is the limiting case — the product is a harness exposed as a library. Among orchestrators, wrapping is the norm and has two idioms: argv-template wrapping of pinned CLIs (Vibe Kanban's per-agent Rust executors, Claude Squad's tmux program strings, Conductor's four harnesses) and, newer, protocol wrapping — OpenHands' ACPAgent speaks the Agent Client Protocol (JSON-RPC over stdio, 40+ implementing agents, Zed-stewarded) so competitors' harnesses run inside its UI on the user's own subscriptions.

QuorumBring-your-own-harness by argv template, with per-harness adapters only at the hook/injection edges. The harness is a black box; quorum owns nothing of the loop.

Verdict: hold, and watch ACP. BYOH is validated — the industry named the layer quorum treats as pluggable. An ACP adapter would be one integration that covers dozens of agents at once; it is the strongest candidate for the next harness integration (see implication #5).

AXIS 02

Control-flow authority

HOLD

Who decides what runs next, and where policy lives. The framework family splits cleanly: typed graphs and code-resident policy (LangGraph's StateGraph, Mastra's schema-typed steps, CrewAI Flows) versus LLM-decides idioms (OpenAI handoffs, smolagents, CrewAI's hierarchical crews). The research record adjudicates: nobody serious still advocates free-form LLM group chat (AutoGen's speaker-selection idiom); the live debate is typed workflow (MetaGPT lineage — coherent but rigid) versus LLM supervisor with hard rails (Anthropic's research system: policy in the prompt, budgets and scaling rules as rails). A 2026 line of work makes coordination a separately configurable layer, swappable without touching agents or tools — which is literally what a policy file like prompts/manager.md is. Among products, human control dominates the OSS tier (kanban, TUI); only Factory and Devin ship a conversational LLM orchestrator.

QuorumThe manager makes no decisions in Python. Policy is a prompt; the substrate carries rails (the action cap) but never judgment. Status is free-form; only terminal statuses mean anything.

Verdict: hold — this is quorum's best-supported stance. The survey found the design implemented elsewhere only at the frontier (Factory, Devin) and endorsed in principle by the 2026 coordination-layer literature. MAST's failure data (~42% of multi-agent failures are specification defects) says the highest-leverage investment is the quality of the prompt and digest, not more orchestration machinery.

AXIS 03

State substrate

HOLD

Where durable truth lives. Frameworks checkpoint into DBs (LangGraph's Postgres/SQLite savers, CrewAI's SQLite @persist); GUI orchestrators independently landed on SQLite (Vibe Kanban, Crystal, Sculptor — the last as an immutable event log with materialized views); cloud products keep opaque service state. But plain files keep recurring at the substrate level, and in strong company: OpenHands V1 persists an event-sourced log of individual JSON files and recovers by replay; container-use uses git itself as the database (branches as environments, git notes as container state); Claude Squad is pure JSON; and most strikingly, Claude Code's experimental agent teams coordinate full sessions through plain local JSON mailboxes and a file-locked shared task list — the same file-substrate design quorum uses, shipped inside the flagship harness in Feb 2026.

QuorumEverything is a file under QUORUM_HOME: atomic-rename writes, JSONL journals and transcripts, maildir inboxes. ls and cat are debuggers; copying the directory migrates the system.

Verdict: hold, strengthened. The pattern quorum bet on now has an existence proof inside Claude Code itself. The one idea worth borrowing without changing substrate: OpenHands' event-log framing (immutable events, one mutable state object) is a useful lens on task.json + append-only JSONL — quorum is already most of the way there.

AXIS 04

Process model & scheduling

HOLD

How work outlives a process. The 2025–26 framework consensus is durable execution by delegation: checkpoint/replay through an external engine (OpenAI documents Temporal/Dapr/Restate/DBOS; Pydantic AI co-maintains Temporal/DBOS/Prefect backends; Mastra ships durable: true). Products deliver survive-laptop-shutdown as vendor cloud: Cursor's Firecracker microVM per agent, Amp's orbs, Codex cloud, Claude Code's --teleport, Copilot's 59-minute Actions runners, Jules' VMs. Locally, most orchestrators kill agent processes with the app (Vibe Kanban reaps orphans at startup); Claude Squad's detached tmux sessions and Sculptor's crash-surviving turns are the exceptions.

QuorumOne ordinary scheduler process; runs are detached children that survive supervisor restarts. No engine, no checkpoints — recovery is the next tick re-reading files. No analogue exists in the framework family.

Verdict: hold. Checkpoint/replay engines solve a problem quorum defined away: a harness run is already a resumable unit (session ids), and the world-state is already durable (files, git). The unanswered story is remote: every vendor now has a laptop-closed answer and quorum doesn't — but the seam is clean (a runner host is just a machine with QUORUM_HOME synced), and Factory's BYOM (register your own box behind a relay) is the shape to study if demand appears. Not urgent; recorded as a watch item.

AXIS 05

Isolation & execution surface

HOLD

Where the agent is allowed to act. The clearest convergence in the survey: worktrees won locally, containers retreated to the cloud tier. Every surviving local orchestrator (Vibe Kanban, Claude Squad, Crystal, uzi, Conductor, Sculptor) isolates with a git worktree + branch per task; Sculptor flipped from container-per-agent (2025) to worktrees-in-your-repo (2026 default). Container-grade isolation reappears as cloud microVMs (Cursor, Conductor Cloud, Devin, Ona — whose environment layer OpenAI bought outright in June 2026). Meanwhile kernel sandboxing became a first-party harness feature: Claude Code (Seatbelt/bubblewrap + network proxy), Codex (bubblewrap + seccomp, Landlock demoted to legacy fallback), Factory ("denied by the kernel rather than relying on Droid to police itself"). Frameworks outsource isolation to a shared sandbox-vendor pool (E2B, Modal, Daytona).

QuorumWorktree + branch per task (shared object store), plus optional fail-closed kernel sandboxing via nono (Landlock/Seatbelt) at supervisor, task, and LLM-call granularity.

Verdict: hold, strengthened. Quorum independently landed on both winning layers (worktrees for collision-avoidance, kernel enforcement for authority limits). One footnote: Codex's move off Landlock to bubblewrap+seccomp is worth tracking for nono's Linux story.

AXIS 06

Safety rails & authority limits

ADOPT +

This is quorum's most contrarian axis. The field runs on typed gates: the Claude Agent SDK evaluates every tool call through a six-step pipeline (hooks → deny → ask → mode → allow → callback, including an LLM classifier mode); Factory classifies per-tool risk against four autonomy levels and ships two fine-tuned models solely to gate git pushes (Droid Shield); OpenHands rates every action LOW→HIGH via a security analyzer; opencode even models runaway loops as a permissioned action (doom_loop: deny). Nothing surveyed uses an action-cap-as-only-rail design. The nearest relatives are budget-class caps: Devin's per-child ACU limits, Anthropic's effort budgets, Codex's concurrency caps — rate limits that bound blast radius without vetoing choices. Two currents favor quorum's stance: rails migrated downward (into kernels and harness permission systems, below the orchestrator), and the 2026 literature's "rails in the substrate, judgment in the prompt" framing is quorum's exact split.

QuorumThe per-run action cap is the only rail — a rate limit, never a veto. Vetoes exist only as substrate rails (runner.lock, the attached-task refusal), never as supervision policy.

Verdict: hold the philosophy, widen the rail family. The cap-not-veto stance survives because the veto layer now lives where it belongs — inside harness permission systems and kernel sandboxes quorum already composes with. But the survey legitimizes more rails of the same class: a token/cost budget per run or task (Devin's ACU caps prove the pattern) alongside the action cap. See implication #4.

AXIS 07

Communication topology

HOLD

How agents talk. Frameworks offer typed handoffs (OpenAI), typed conversation channels (AG2's auto-closing "consulting" channel), and MCP everywhere. Products, strikingly, have no peer message bus at all — coordination is hierarchical (Factory/Devin orchestrator→worker), indirect (shared filesystem), or absent; convergence happens at the human merge gate. The research record explains the winning shape: MetaGPT's shared pub-sub message pool with subscription filtering beat ChatDev's conversational relay decisively — structured artifacts over dialogue, a blackboard over chat. And the strongest recent data point is again Claude Code agent teams: peer coordination through JSON file mailboxes and a file-locked task list. On the protocol layer, A2A and ACP are rising (Gemini CLI mounts remote agents by agent-card URL as first-class subagents), while rich session control-planes fragment per vendor.

QuorumOne message schema, two channels: an append-only board (cursor-based readers, no consumption marks) and maildir inboxes (atomic rename claims, delivery-is-acknowledgment). No new transports; herdr is a doorbell, never a second transport.

Verdict: hold. The board is MetaGPT's message pool built from files; the maildir claim protocol is more careful than anything surveyed (Claude Code's team mailboxes use file locks, not atomic claims). The outbox-and-router seam already recorded in the architecture doc remains the right dormant answer to cross-sandbox delivery.

AXIS 08

Human steering & session adoption

ADOPT

Mid-run steering of a live session became a wire primitive everywhere in 2026: Claude Code stream-json stdin turns and --cloud nudges; Codex turn/steer and turn/interrupt as JSON-RPC calls on its app-server; Amp's steer: true queued messages; opencode's prompt_async/abort HTTP endpoints; Copilot's live steering prompts; Jules' send-message endpoint. Frameworks converged on the durable-pause idiom (LangGraph interrupt()/resume, serialized RunState approvals). Takeover is richest at Cursor (VM desktop control, pull-to-IDE). One capability appears nowhere else in the survey: adopting a pre-existing interactive session — recording the human's own live harness session as a supervised task via hooks. Omnara relays sessions to a phone; nothing else inverts ownership the way task adopt does.

QuorumGuidancePump injects inbox messages as stream-json user turns mid-run; task nudge reaches attached sessions at their next stop via harness hooks; task adopt makes the human's live session a first-class supervised task.

Verdict: adopt the new steering planes; adoption is a moat. Quorum's injection currently rides one protocol (Claude-style stream-json — which Amp deliberately cloned). Codex's app-server and opencode's HTTP API now offer equivalent or better steering hooks; thin per-harness steering adapters would extend mid-run guidance to the whole roster (implication #1). Session adoption is genuinely differentiated — worth naming loudly in positioning.

AXIS 09

Failure semantics & recovery

ADOPT +

Two recovery philosophies: checkpoint/replay (the framework consensus; OpenHands' deterministic event-log replay is the purest) versus re-read the world (stateless recovery from durable ground truth). Watchdogs are the interesting sub-pattern: OpenHands ships a stuck detector (sliding-window hash comparison on repeated actions, auto-halt); opencode declares doom-loops a permissioned action; Sculptor's CI Babysitter polls for red checks and spawns capped fix-it agents; Jules' CI Fixer auto-remediates its own PRs. On degraded modes, the field mostly degrades silently; quorum's fail-loud, no-fallback supervision has no peer — and needs none, since its auto-pause exemption makes recovery automatic.

QuorumFail loudly, recover automatically: the manager's tick raises visibly without a working harness, and the schedule keeps firing so the first good tick re-reads files and reinvokes what's needed. No degraded supervision by design.

Verdict: hold the philosophy; adopt cheap loop observation. The anti-loop memory (journal with then-vs-now outcomes) covers the manager's loops. Nothing yet observes a task harness spinning — OpenHands-style repetition detection over transcript tails is a cheap, pure-file enrichment to the digest (an observation for the manager to act on, never a veto), and fits the passive-observation stance exactly. See implication #3.

AXIS 10

Verification & trust

ADOPT

The line through the literature is monotone: dialogic review under-verifies (ChatDev), executable feedback works (MetaGPT), verification is the terminal failure category (MAST), and agents don't self-report honestly — the 2026 field study found inaccurate self-reporting rising while 91.5% of misalignment fixes required explicit human correction. Products responded by productizing review: Factory's rubric-based review droids, Devin Review with auto-fix, Copilot's first-line agent review, Amp's Oracle (a standing second-opinion agent on a different frontier model), Imbue's Vet (checks agent actions against conversation history), and CI-reactive janitors (Sculptor's Babysitter, Jules' CI Fixer, Cursor's auto-fix of failing Actions). Best-of-N generate-then-select (Codex --attempts, Jules --parallel, Crystal's A/B sessions) is the other productized verification shape.

QuorumGround-truth probes over self-report: workdir_git_state flags stranded work (dirty/unpushed), and the default manager prompt treats it as not-done and relaunches. Transcripts and locks give passive observation of uncooperative harnesses.

Verdict: adopt — this is the highest-leverage axis. The stranded-work probe is exactly what the literature prescribes, and it should grow teeth toward CI: a PR/checks probe in the digest (via gh, fail-soft like herdr) plus a shipped babysitter-style prompt agent would close the loop from "pushed" to "green" (implication #2). A reviewer prompt agent is nearly free — the substrate already supports it; policy is just another prompt file.

AXIS 11

Memory & context engineering

HOLD

The 2025–26 literature made context a first-class engineering surface: finite attention budgets and context rot (Anthropic), full traces over summaries (Cognition), and — most relevantly — two 2026 results on long-horizon memory: ACE (contexts as evolving playbooks maintained by incremental delta updates; iterative full rewrites cause "context collapse") and MemAct (memory curation as explicit actions). Both validate append-only, outcome-annotated, incrementally-updated journals and condemn summarize-and-rewrite compaction. Just-in-time retrieval — fetch identifiers at runtime rather than preloading — is the emerging idiom for keeping working context small.

QuorumThe digest is assembled fresh each tick from ground truth (statuses, liveness, transcript tails, git state) plus the manager's append-only action journal with then-vs-now outcomes — an ACE-style playbook by construction, with zero accumulated context to rot.

Verdict: hold. The digest design anticipates the 2026 consensus almost exactly. The one tension: Cognition's full-traces-beat-summaries argues transcript tails can under-inform — but quorum's manager runs with cwd=QUORUM_HOME and can read any full transcript on demand, which is just-in-time retrieval. Worth one line in the manager prompt encouraging it when a tail looks ambiguous; no mechanism change.

Implications for quorum, ranked by leverage

No finding argues for revising a recorded stance — the big three invariants and the smaller per-layer stances all came back strengthened. Everything below is additive, ordered by expected payoff over effort.

#1

Steering adapters for the new control planes

ADOPT

Mid-run injection currently requires inject = "stream-json" — one protocol. Codex's app-server (turn/steer, turn/interrupt over JSON-RPC), opencode's HTTP API (prompt_async, abort), and Amp's Claude-compatible steer: true now offer equivalent injection points. Thin per-harness steering adapters — same GuidancePump contract, different wire — would extend mid-run guidance from one harness to the whole roster.

Why now: every vendor made sessions addressable in 2026; the adapter surface is thin and has stabilized by imitation of Claude Code.

#2

Extend ground-truth probes to CI, and ship a babysitter

ADOPT

The stranded-work probe stops at "pushed." A fail-soft PR/checks probe (via gh, herdr-style: absent tooling degrades to nothing) would put red CI into the digest; a shipped example prompt agent in the Sculptor-Babysitter shape (watch checks on quorum-branch PRs, nudge or relaunch the task with the failure) closes the loop from "pushed" to "green". Policy stays in a prompt file — no new Python decisions.

Why now: the strongest empirical finding in the survey — agents don't self-detect failure (91.5% of fixes were human-initiated), and every serious product grew a CI-reactive loop in 2026.

#3

Loop observation in the digest

ADOPT

An OpenHands-style repetition heuristic (sliding-window hashing over transcript-tail tool calls) surfacing a possible-loop flag per task in the digest. Strictly an observation for the manager to act on — never an automatic halt — preserving the cap-is-the-only-rail stance.

Why now: cheap, pure-file, and covers the one loop class the action journal can't see: a task harness spinning inside a single run.

#4

Budget-class rails beside the action cap

ADOPT

A per-run token/cost budget (harnesses now report usage in their result events — total_cost_usd in Claude's, token counts in Codex's) as a second rate-limit-class rail. Same philosophy as the action cap: bounds blast radius, never second-guesses a choice.

Why now: Devin's per-child ACU caps and Anthropic's effort budgets legitimized the pattern; token usage explains 80% of multi-agent performance variance, so cost is the honest thing to meter.

#5

An ACP adapter as the universal harness integration

WATCH → ADOPT

The Agent Client Protocol (JSON-RPC over stdio, 40+ agents, Linux Foundation-adjacent stewardship, public registry) is how OpenHands wraps competitors' harnesses. One ACP adapter in integrations/ could subsume many per-CLI templates — worth prototyping once a second orchestrator-relevant harness ships ACP support natively.

Why watch: convergence is real but young; the argv-template path still covers the harnesses that matter today.

#6

Best-of-N tasks

WATCH

Fan one task to N worktrees, let the manager (or human) pick the winner — Codex --attempts, Jules --parallel, and Crystal's A/B flow all productized this. Quorum's substrate nearly supports it already (N tasks, one prompt); the missing piece is a comparison affordance, not orchestration.

Why only watch: it multiplies token cost by N for wins the literature attributes mostly to the extra compute; let user demand pull it.

#7

Positioning: say the differentiated things out loud

ADOPT

Two capabilities appear nowhere else in a 30-project survey: session adoption (the human's live session as a supervised task) and harness-driven supervision on a plain-file substrate (the frontier products' pattern, local-first and inspectable). The README should claim both explicitly, framed by the survey's own conclusion: as harnesses absorb orchestration mechanics, the external layer's value concentrates in being cross-harness, local-first, and policy-owned.

Why now: the framing writes itself from primary sources, and the crowded middle of this market just died — differentiation is the survival trait.
